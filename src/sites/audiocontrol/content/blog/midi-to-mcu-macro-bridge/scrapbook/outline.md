## Section structure

Six sections. Magazine dividers (`---`). Sentence-case headings. Demo video above the fold; sections build the case from the wider DAWless framing inward to the MC-500mkII as the worked example, then outward again to "where this goes next."

### § 01 — Get away from the laptop. Don't give up the DAW.

Lead with the DAWless impulse, plainly: a lot of musicians want to make music with their hands on something other than a trackpad. Hardware that sits on the desk, lit up, ready, the way an instrument is ready. That's why the DAWless movement exists. The trade-off most people accept: you give up some of what the DAW does well — the unlimited tracks, the plug-in chains, the recall.

What if you don't have to give that up? What if a beautiful piece of vintage hardware drove the DAW from across the desk?

That's the dispatch in two paragraphs. The third paragraph names the MC-500mkII:

> The Roland MC-500mkII (1988) is the kind of box that makes the case for itself. More cash register than equipment panel — buttons under your fingertips, a data wheel with real weight, a screen the size of a postage stamp because you don't need a big screen when the controls are this clear. Eight tracks of MIDI, 99 location memories, and the kind of build quality that means it's been quietly doing its job for thirty-eight years on someone's desk.

Close with the move: I built a way to make the MC-500mkII drive a modern DAW. Hands on the box. Eyes on the room. The DAW does what the DAW does well; the box does what the box does well.

### § 02 — What it actually does (musician-language)

Operator's framing (v4): *a way to use the MC-500mkII as a control surface for your DAW.*

"Control surface" is the term — universally understood by musicians, even those who don't know what MCU is. The MC-500mkII becomes a control surface for whatever DAW you live in.

**Screenshot placeholder:** §02 needs an image of the bridge's control / configuration surface (operator's v4 note). Image to be captured at draft time and dropped at `./control-surface.png` (or similar).

You hit play on the box, the DAW plays. You spin the wheel, the DAW jogs. You recall a location, the DAW seeks. That's the dispatch.

### § 03 — What you get from the console

Transport buttons drive the DAW transport. The data wheel becomes the jog wheel. The 99 location memories become jump-to-marker buttons.

The location memories are the part to reread. Pre-configure verse, chorus, bridge as MC-500 locations. One button on the console jumps the DAW to that bar. The MC-500mkII becomes a tactile arrangement-jumping interface for whatever DAW is on the other end. Years of muscle memory finally pay rent.

### § 04 — The moment it works (in case you skipped the video)

Self-contained description for readers who didn't watch the embed above. Operator's v7 note: *"Don't assume the reader actually watched the video."*

Setup paragraph: the MC-500mkII sits on the desk next to the laptop. The bridge is running. The MC-500 is connected to the computer via MIDI; the laptop runs LUNA (or Logic, or Live). The MC-500's panel is, at this moment, a control surface for the DAW.

Then the action, paragraph by paragraph (each sentence carrying the next thing the reader sees if they cued the video):

- Hit play on the MC-500's transport. LUNA's transport rolls. The DAW's playhead moves; the meters light up; the takes you laid down play back. Hands never touched the trackpad.
- Spin the data wheel on the MC-500. LUNA's playhead jogs in the timeline — fine resolution if you turn it slowly, faster scrub if you spin it. The same wheel that's been quietly editing MC-500 sequences since 1988, now scrubbing your modern session.
- Recall a location memory on the MC-500 (one of 99 you can pre-configure). LUNA's playhead seeks to that bar instantly. Pre-configure verse / chorus / bridge as locations on the MC-500; the panel becomes a tactile arrangement-jumping interface for the DAW.

Close the section with the VO line inline:

> *"hit play on the MC-500, and you get the transport, which I think is pretty great."*

(Reader hasn't necessarily watched the video. After reading this section, they don't need to — they have the picture in language. The video is for those who want to *see* it; this section is the description that stands on its own.)

### § 05 — What's tested

Three bolded lead-ins. Audiocontrol's "honest about limits" register, sharper now per v6 corrections:

- **Hardware.** Roland MC-500mkII (1988) — confirmed. The Roland MC-50 (1989, smaller, cheaper, same family) — likely works, but I haven't tested it. The original Roland MC-500 (1986) — *does not work* (different operating system, different transport behavior). If you have an MC-50 and try it, let me know.
- **DAWs.** Logic Pro, Ableton Live, LUNA. If your DAW takes a hardware control surface for transport, this is one. If it doesn't, this isn't.
- **Computer.** Mac. The Linux build runs but I haven't tested it against a DAW. No Windows yet.

### § 06 — Get it / what's next

The macOS download is a disk image containing an `.app`. Mount the disk image, drag the `.app` into your Applications folder. Open it, point your DAW at it as a control surface, and you're running.

→ Download: <https://github.com/audiocontrol-org/audiocontrol/releases>

What's next: this dispatch is part of a series. **Already working:** Novation LaunchControlXL mk3 driving LUNA's mixer — Novation doesn't ship LUNA support out of the box; we do. A separate dispatch (with video) on that one is in flight. After that: more vintage gear, more DAWs, more of the panel mapped, as bench needs surface them.

If you've got an MC-500mkII and a DAW, try it. If it breaks on your setup, file an issue with what gear and what DAW.

*If you want the protocol details — MIDI Song Position, Mackie Control, the bridge architecture — the source is at <https://github.com/audiocontrol-org/audiocontrol>.* (One italic line at the end of §06; operator confirmed: *"Keep. It's a good idea to explain to the nerds how it works."*)
